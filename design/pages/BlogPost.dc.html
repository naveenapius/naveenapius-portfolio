Shipping Enduro Edge: bookings without the bloat

JUN 20268 MIN READ

When an off-road events company asked me for "a website," what they actually needed was a way to fill riding slots and stop chasing payments over WhatsApp. Enduro Edge is what happened once I ignored the brief and solved that instead.

The temptation on a project like this is to build the thing that demos well: a slick marketing site with parallax and a blog nobody asked for. But the client's pain was operational, not cosmetic. Riders would DM to ask about dates, go quiet, and half of them never paid. Every unbooked slot was money left on the trail.

The real problem

So I reframed it. The site had exactly two jobs: take a booking with payment up front, and capture a lead when someone wasn't ready to commit. Everything else — galleries, testimonials, the founder's story — was allowed to exist only if it pushed toward one of those two actions.

Every feature I said no to is a feature I don't have to debug at 11 p.m. before a ride weekend.

What I built

The stack stayed deliberately boring — boring is exactly what you want when real money moves through it:

Next.js on Vercel — server-rendered pages, one deploy pipeline, no server to babysit.
Sanity for event content the client edits themselves — dates, prices, photos.
Razorpay + Upstash — payments, with an idempotency key so a double-tap never double-charges.
NocoDB as the ops view, so the team reads bookings in a spreadsheet-shaped thing they already understand.

What I'd do again

Validate every booking on the server, treat the payment webhook as the source of truth, and log enough that when something looks wrong you can answer "what happened" without redeploying. None of it is clever. All of it is why the phone doesn't ring on ride weekends.
